# Adversarial Quiz Audit

Audit date: 30 July 2026

Scope: practical usefulness for two first-time trekkers once they are on trail. This is an omission and false-confidence audit, not a copyedit.

## Verdict

The original 80-question deck was broad and generally conservative, but it behaved more like a study deck than a readiness assessment. Its largest risk was the score:

- Any short response containing a few keywords could pass even when it negated the safe action or used dangerous units.
- Two model answers supplied by the deck failed their own keyword grader because of plural or suffix differences.
- Any 12-character scenario response could be self-certified after the model answer was shown.
- A one-question missed-item review could create a permanent 100% section “mastery” score.
- A high total could hide a failed crossing, SOS, hypothermia, wildfire, or navigation decision.
- The nominally two-person quiz did not test pair communication, role transfer, vetoes, or whether both people could make a call independently.

Those are measurement defects, not cosmetic issues. “Certification,” “mastery,” and “trail-ready answer” language was removed.

## Changes made

### Assessment mechanics

- Added a **Duo field drill** with 16 shuffled scenarios. Caller and assessor roles alternate every question.
- Replaced binary scenario self-certification with explicit required-criterion checkboxes. A scenario passes only when every criterion actually stated before reveal is checked.
- Applied the same rubric method to free-text answers, removing the unsafe keyword grader from the user flow.
- Added non-compensatory **must-pass** questions. Any critical miss blocks the readiness verdict regardless of total percentage.
- Quick mode now guarantees one core question from every section before filling the remaining slots randomly.
- Quick, duo, and missed-item practice cannot update section results. Only a complete eight-question section or comprehensive run can do so.
- Saved section results are the latest complete result, with date/version metadata, rather than the highest score from any sample.
- Comprehensive, quick, missed, and duo queues are interleaved so the section label does less work as an answer cue.
- Duo results show each hiker’s caller score and must-pass gaps separately.

### Question-bank changes

The bank remains 80 questions, ten sections, eight questions per section, and eight formats. Low-yield or duplicated items were replaced with:

- day-one pace, pack rub, hot-spot, and optional-mileage decisions;
- terrain-versus-GPS disagreement and two-app conflicts;
- lightning casualty care;
- worsening smoke plus respiratory symptoms;
- tent/vestibule stove safety;
- river-crossing consent and shore-based decision conditions;
- cold-water swim setup and watcher roles;
- wet/windy two-person shelter roles;
- nighttime pad/condensation/cold-system failure;
- visual campsite-hazard identification;
- food-spill cleanup and the rule that current storage orders override remembered quiz details;
- fresh bear sign and carcass avoidance;
- an initial patient assessment and life-threatening bleeding control;
- failed satellite-message confirmation;
- triggering SOS for an incapacitated device owner;
- partner vetoes, post-SOS operations, late-day camp decisions, body-margin gates, and stale infrastructure information.

## Online quiz and course patterns reviewed

Questions were not copied verbatim. The useful concepts and interaction patterns were adapted into original Section J scenarios.

- [NPS Isle Royale Wilderness Adventure](https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/isle-royale-wilderness-adventure.htm): branching consequences and whole-team decisions.
- [NPS trip-planning guide](https://www.nps.gov/subjects/healthandsafety/trip-planning-guide.htm): stay together, self-check during the trip, and switch to a backup plan.
- [NPS safe river crossings](https://www.nps.gov/articles/safe-river-crossings.htm): Plan, Pick, Assess, Check; inspect the runout and preserve retreat.
- [NPS two-player trail game](https://www.nps.gov/seki/planyourvisit/lntathome.htm): alternating guide and responder roles inspired the duo drill.
- [NOLS wilderness-medicine case studies](https://www.nols.edu/category/wilderness-medicine/case-studies/) and [initial assessment](https://www.nols.edu/blog/abcs-of-wilderness-medicine-the-initial-assessment/): assess incomplete information before naming a diagnosis.
- [Leave No Trace quizzes](https://lnt.org/our-work/research/quizzes/) and [backcountry course](https://learn.lnt.org/courses/backcountry): short teach-then-check units on campsites, waste, food storage, wildlife, and etiquette.
- [NWS lightning safety](https://www.weather.gov/safety/lightning-sports): a casualty is safe to touch and may need immediate CPR/AED care.
- [Red Cross life-threatening bleeding guidance](https://www.redcross.org/take-a-class/resources/learn-first-aid/bleeding-life-threatening-external) and [ACS Stop the Bleed](https://www.stopthebleed.org/get-trained/online-course/): recognize dangerous flow, apply immediate pressure, communicate, and use advanced control methods only as trained.
- [Garmin device-test guidance](https://www8.garmin.com/manuals/webhelp/explorewebsitepro/EN-US/GUID-B6CD3E55-679F-417A-BDFE-761E7F50DA3E.html): open sky, correct orientation, stillness, and actual confirmation.
- [CDC heat-illness guidance](https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/heat-stress/about/illnesses.html): altered mental status is the operational trigger and sweating does not rule out heat stroke.
- [CDC hypothermia guidance](https://www.cdc.gov/winter-weather/prevention/index.html): cold rain, wet clothing, confusion, fumbling, and slurred speech can signal an emergency above freezing.

## Residual limitations

- A browser quiz cannot demonstrate a correct shelter pitch, tourniquet, river-crossing method, CPR, map bearing, bear hang, or messenger workflow.
- River-crossing technique details differ across reputable training systems. The revised questions test the no-cross decision, consent, runout, retreat, and use of a practiced method rather than pretending one pack-strap rule is universal.
- Medical and SOS procedures vary by training, device, patient, and incident. The quiz teaches recognition, first priorities, and evacuation thresholds; it does not replace hands-on wilderness first aid, CPR/AED, bleeding-control, river-safety, or device training.
- Current closures, bridge status, fire orders, food-storage orders, weather, smoke, water, and trail damage expire. Current official evidence always overrides a remembered answer.

The correct use of the quiz is: learn by section, run the duo drill, clear all must-pass gaps independently, then demonstrate the physical procedures with the exact equipment the pair will carry.
